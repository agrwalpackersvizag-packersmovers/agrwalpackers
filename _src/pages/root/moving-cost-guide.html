Moving Cost Guide: How Packers and Movers Charges Are Calculated

We do not publish a rate card, because a price for a home nobody has surveyed is either padded or about to change. What we can do is explain exactly which factors move the number, so you can read any quote — ours or a competitor's — and know what you are looking at.

The short version

Local moves are priced mainly on labour and access. Intercity moves are priced mainly on volume and distance. Packing material is a real, consumable cost in both. Everything else is a modifier.

The ten factors that decide your price

1. Distance

Distance behaves differently at different scales. Inside a city, the drive is a minor part of the day and has little effect on the total. Between cities, distance drives fuel, tolls, driver time and vehicle utilisation, and becomes one of the two dominant costs. A 300 km move and a 1,800 km move are different products, not different sizes of the same one.

2. Volume of goods

Intercity moving is sold by the space your goods take up in the truck, measured in cubic feet. Weight rarely decides anything, because a truck runs out of space long before it runs out of capacity. This is why decluttering before the survey is the most effective cost reduction available to you — every carton you do not send is volume you do not pay for.

3. Number and type of items

A headcount of rooms is a poor proxy for a move. What matters is the specific inventory: how many wardrobes, whether the dining table is glass or stone-topped, whether there is a piano, a treadmill, a large aquarium, a double-door refrigerator or a six-seater sofa that cannot be dismantled. Each of these changes crew size, handling time and sometimes the vehicle.

4. Floor level

Every flight of stairs is carrying time, and carrying time is the labour line on your invoice. A ground-floor move and a fourth-floor move of identical goods are genuinely different jobs. Movers price this explicitly, and a quote that does not ask which floor you live on has not priced it at all.

5. Lift availability — and lift usability

Two separate questions. Does the building have a lift, and will the building let you use it for a move? Many societies restrict moves to a service lift, restrict the hours, or require a booking. Then there is size: a passenger lift that will not take a three-seater sofa or a double-door refrigerator means those pieces go down the stairs regardless of the lift existing.

6. Access and parking distance

How close can the truck legally and physically get to your door? Where it cannot get close — a narrow lane, a no-entry restriction, a gated campus with a vehicle size limit — the move needs a shuttle: a smaller vehicle running between the house and the truck. That is extra labour, extra vehicle and extra time, and it is one of the biggest cost surprises for people who did not have a survey.

7. Packing requirements

Packing scope is a choice, and it has a real price attached:

  • Full packing — everything, including clothes, books and kitchen consumables. Highest labour and material, lowest effort for you.
  • Partial packing — we handle fragile items, appliances and furniture; you pack clothes, books and linen. A meaningful saving for most households.
  • Fragile-only — crockery, glass, mirrors, art and electronics only. The rest is yours.

See packing services for what each scope includes.

8. Vehicle type and size

The vehicle is chosen to match volume, not preference. Undersizing it means a second trip — which costs more than the larger vehicle would have. Oversizing it means paying for empty space. This is exactly what a survey is measuring.

9. Moving date

Demand is not evenly spread. Indian tenancies overwhelmingly turn over at month-end, so the last and first few days of a month are peak, as are weekends and the weeks around major festivals. On those dates every mover in the city is short of crews, and prices reflect it. Mid-month and mid-week gives you more choice and better rates.

10. Special handling and insurance

Some items need more than careful wrapping. Wooden crating for a glass tabletop, marble or sandstone slab, artwork or a musical instrument. Hoisting through a window or balcony where a piece will not fit down the staircase. Dismantling of a modular wardrobe that was assembled in situ. Each of these is a separate, quotable line.

Goods-in-transit cover is also separate, priced against the value you declare. It is optional. It should be explained to you, not sold as a vague promise that everything is “fully insured”.

How to compare two moving quotes properly

Comparing headline totals tells you almost nothing. Quotes differ in what they include, and the cheapest number on the page is frequently the one with the most exclusions. Work through this list on each quote:

  • Is packing material included, and is the packing scope stated (full, partial, fragile-only)?
  • Is dismantling and reassembly of furniture included?
  • Is unloading included at the destination, or only loading at origin?
  • Is unpacking included, and if so, of everything or only selected cartons?
  • Is a shuttle vehicle included if a truck cannot reach either door?
  • What is the transit window, and is it dedicated or shared transport?
  • Are tolls, permits and any state entry taxes included?
  • Is goods-in-transit cover included, offered, or not mentioned at all?
  • Are waiting charges specified, and what triggers them?
  • Is there a written inventory, and who signs it at both ends?

A quote that answers all ten is more useful than a quote that is ten per cent cheaper and answers none.

Where the cost differs by city

The same household costs different amounts to move in each of the cities we serve, for reasons that are structural rather than arbitrary.

  • Jodhpur — shuttle vehicles are needed more often than elsewhere, because much of the old city and several older colonies cannot take a large truck. Heavy stone furniture is also more common here than in most cities, and it changes crew size.
  • Noida — high-rise societies restrict move timings and service-lift access, which compresses the working window and can push a move into a second day. Delhi-NCR commercial vehicle restrictions also affect when a truck can travel.
  • Visakhapatnam — coastal humidity means more moisture-barrier material per carton, and the hill approaches around Madhurawada and Rushikonda limit vehicle size on some roads.

What we will never do on price

  • Quote a firm price without seeing what is being moved.
  • Present a low headline figure with the intention of revising it once loading has started.
  • Describe transit cover as included when it is a separate, optional product.
  • Charge for a larger vehicle than the survey justified.
