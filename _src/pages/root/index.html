How it works, from enquiry to handover

Six steps. You know what happens next at every one of them.

  1. Tell us what you are moving

    A phone call, a WhatsApp message or the quote form. We need the origin, the destination, a rough date and the size of the home or office.

  2. Pre-move survey

    A surveyor walks the property, or you walk us through it on a video call. This is where the real inventory, the floor level, the lift size and the parking access get recorded.

  3. Written estimate

    You get the scope in writing: vehicle size, crew size, packing material, what is included and what is not. Questions get answered before you book, not after.

  4. Booking and date lock

    The date is confirmed and the crew is assigned. If your building needs a gate pass or a lift booking, this is when we tell you what to arrange.

  5. Packing and loading

    Packing is done room by room and boxes are labelled by room and content. Loading follows an order that puts the heavy, stable items in first.

  6. Delivery and unpacking

    Goods are checked against the inventory as they come off the truck, furniture is reassembled, and the boxes you want opened are unpacked before the crew leaves.

What actually happens on moving day

The survey decides the price. The moving day decides whether the move goes well. Here is the sequence a trained crew follows, and why the order matters.

Fragile and breakable first. Crockery, glass, mirrors and decorative pieces are packed at the start of the day, while the crew is fresh and the house is still calm. Each piece is individually wrapped, and cartons are filled so nothing can shift inside.

Appliances next. Refrigerators need to stand upright and be defrosted the night before. Washing machines need the drum secured. Televisions travel in corner-protected cartons and never flat.

Furniture is dismantled, not forced. Bed frames, dining tables, wardrobes and modular units come apart. Fittings go into labelled bags that are taped to the piece they belong to — the single most common reason a reassembly goes wrong is a missing bag of bolts.

Loading is a stacking problem. Heavy, square and stable goes to the floor of the truck. Soft goods fill voids. Fragile cartons ride on top and never carry weight. Everything is strapped so the load cannot move on the first sharp turn.

Unloading is the inventory check. Boxes come off against the list, go to the room written on the label, and furniture is reassembled before the crew leaves. Damage, if any, is recorded at that moment rather than discovered a week later.

What decides the cost of your move

We do not publish fixed prices, because a fixed price for an unseen home is either padded or about to be revised. These are the factors that actually move the number.

  • Distance — a cross-town move and a cross-country move are different products, not different sizes of the same one.
  • Volume — intercity pricing follows the space your goods occupy in the truck, not their weight.
  • Number and type of items — a piano, a treadmill or a five-door wardrobe changes crew size and handling time.
  • Floor level and lift availability — a fourth floor with no service lift can add hours of carrying.
  • Packing scope — full packing, partial packing or fragile-only changes both labour and material.
  • Vehicle type — a tempo, a 17-foot truck or a container each carry a different volume and cost.
  • Moving date — month-end, weekends and festival weeks are peak demand for every mover in the city.
  • Special handling — crating, hoisting through a window or a shuttle vehicle for a narrow lane are priced as extras.
  • Insurance — transit cover is optional, priced on declared value, and never bundled in silently.
